02Research

Codesign of Energy Systems

Overview and three research areas.

01Overview

Most research optimizes either how a system is built or how it is operated — rarely both at once. My research is organized around a different premise: that equipment design and operational control are a coupled problem, and solving them together yields outcomes neither can achieve alone. I call this philosophy Codesign. It runs through all three research tracks below — from topology-optimized hardware, to the simulations that evaluate it, to the AI agents that operate it in the field.

02Area 01 — AI-driven industrial energy control

AI-driven industrial energy control

The active direction of the program. Industrial facilities (manufacturing lines, battery dry rooms, large HVAC plants) run today on a hierarchy of low-level controllers and rule books that compensate for design choices locked in years earlier. The work climbs that hierarchy: PID → MPC → LLM-based AI agents, with each rung buying something the rung below it could not represent.

Step 01 · Reactive

PID

Cheap, robust, ubiquitous on the plant floor. No anticipation, no explicit constraints, but every operator already trusts it. The realistic baseline.

Step 02 · Predictive

MPC

Model-predictive control buys anticipation and explicit constraint handling. Performance is bounded by model fidelity; for a desiccant-rotor HVAC calibrated against field data, this rung delivered roughly 20% energy savings in our digital-twin study.

Step 03 · Reasoning

AI agent

An LLM-based agent reads plant documentation, reasons over heterogeneous signals, and orchestrates the rungs below. Hard constraints stay in the lower control layers; the agent handles the supervisory decisions that previously required an experienced engineer.

The engineering challenge of the area is connecting large language models to physical PLC infrastructure. A domain ontology gives the agent a vocabulary for the plant’s equipment, signals, and constraints. A thin layer of real-time communication protocols (TCP / UDP) lets the agent observe and command the PLCs that move the actuators. The result is auditable, constraint-aware supervisory control on a real production line, not a chatbot reading historian data.

Underneath the agent sits the energy-efficiency work it depends on: measurement-driven simulations of energy-intensive manufacturing processes (battery electrode drying, steel casting, industrial HVAC) and energy-based key performance indicators that put process designs and operating strategies on the same axis. Without this layer, the agent has nothing honest to optimize against.

Keywords: LLM agents · MPC · PID · ontologies · TCP / UDP · PLC · battery manufacturing · industrial HVAC · electrode drying · steel casting · energy KPIs.

03Area 02 — Codesign of thermal-fluid systems

Codesign of thermal-fluid systems

Hardware-side work: design and operation of thermal-fluid systems at component and plant scale. Topology optimization generates non-intuitive geometries for heat sinks, heat exchangers, and thermal insulators under multi-physics constraints (natural and forced convection, conjugate heat transfer, thermal–structural coupling). This line of work is ongoing and now advanced primarily through student supervision; current explorations include aerogel heat sinks, dual-fluid printed-circuit heat exchangers, and thermoelectric modules with phase-change buffers, all graduate-student led.

The aim is the geometry coupled with the operating policy that will run it, not the geometry alone. A physics-based digital twin of the target plant sits between hardware and control: Simulink and Modelica plant models, CFD-derived reduced-order models, multiscale process couplings, all calibrated against field measurements. The twin has to respond honestly to a change in geometry and a change in supervisory policy, so a hardware revision and a control revision can be argued on the same evidence. The desiccant-rotor twin and the multiscale electrode-drying model are the current examples.

On the runway: codesign of electrode-drying lines, where the transient model-switching in the twin drives both dryer-zone redesign and the drying-schedule controller; topology-optimized heat exchangers whose channel layout is conditioned on the supervisory policy that will run them.

Keywords: topology optimization · heat sinks · heat exchangers · thermal insulators · natural and forced convection · digital twin · Simulink · Modelica · reduced-order modeling · codesign.

04Area 03 — AI / ML for engineering simulation

AI / ML for engineering simulation

Machine-learning methods that accelerate or replace conventional engineering simulations: graph neural networks for convergence acceleration of iterative topology-optimization solvers on unstructured meshes, deep-learned finite elements that embed learned shape functions, surrogate models for expensive multi-physics analyses, and deep reinforcement learning agents that explore large design spaces. The ongoing focus is on methods that generalize across geometries and boundary conditions, so the design and control loops above can run at a budget that first-principles solvers cannot deliver.

This area is the methodological supply line for the other two: the cheap surrogates the topology-optimization and control loops consume, and the convergence-acceleration machinery that makes those inner loops tractable.

Keywords: graph neural networks · deep-learned finite elements · surrogate modeling · deep reinforcement learning · convergence acceleration.
