Codesign of energy systems

01Philosophy

Most research optimizes either how a system is built or how it is run. I am interested in doing both at once — treating design and operation as a single coupled optimization problem. I call this Codesign.

A heat exchanger that is geometrically optimal under nominal load rarely runs at nominal load. A control policy tuned for an off-the-shelf plant rarely uses the plant's actual degrees of freedom. The two choices — what to build and how to run it — are usually made by different people, in sequence, and the seam between them is where most of the achievable energy savings disappear.

The work below approaches that seam as the unit of analysis. Equipment geometry, supervisory control, and the model that connects them are designed against the same objective from the start.

02Research interests

  1. AI agents for industrial energy control

    LLM-based agents that read plant documentation, reason over sensor streams, and drive equipment through an ontology layer down to TCP / UDP and the PLC. The aim is supervisory control that is auditable, constraint-aware, and deployable on real manufacturing lines — not chatbots over historian data.

  2. Energy efficiency of manufacturing processes

    Process-scale modeling and control of energy-intensive operations — battery electrode drying, industrial HVAC, steel casting. Measurement-driven simulations and energy-based KPIs that let process designs and operating strategies be compared on the same axis.

  3. Codesign of thermal systems

    Topology optimization of heat sinks, heat exchangers, and thermal insulators, paired with the operating policy that will run them. Advanced primarily through student supervision; the long-running track that anchors the “what to build” side of Codesign.

03Ongoing projects

Active · 2024 —

LLM-based AI Agent for HVAC Control

Autonomous supervisory control of HVAC for a battery-manufacturing dry room. The agent layer talks to a domain ontology, which talks to PLCs over TCP / UDP. Quality and safety constraints are enforced explicitly; the LLM reasons over deviations, schedules, and operator intent rather than directly closing the loop.

Active · 2023 —

Digital Twin of a Desiccant Rotor HVAC

Simulink-based digital twin of a desiccant-rotor air-handling system, calibrated against field measurements. Used to evaluate control strategies offline; current results show roughly 20% energy savings against the as-built operating policy.

04Open questions

  1. When does codesigning equipment and operation actually beat doing them in sequence — and how do we know in advance, before the hardware is built?

  2. Can an LLM-based agent be made auditable enough to run an industrial line under hard quality and safety constraints, without humans in the loop on every decision?

  3. What is the right interface between an optimal geometry and the policy that will operate it — a learned model, a reduced-order surrogate, or something the controller itself updates online?

  4. How much of energy-intensive manufacturing’s waste is structural — baked into how plants are commissioned — versus operational, and recoverable through better supervisory control?
