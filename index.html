Codesign of energy systems

01Philosophy

Most research optimizes either how a system is built or how it is run, and uses a different model for each. I do the three at once: equipment geometry, operating policy, and the physics-based digital twin that links them, treated as one coupled problem. I call this Codesign.

A heat exchanger that is geometrically optimal under nominal load rarely runs at nominal load. A control policy tuned for an off-the-shelf plant rarely uses the plant’s degrees of freedom. A simulation that captures one of those facts but not the other will make confident, wrong recommendations to both teams. What to build, how to run it, and how to model it: in most labs these are three separate problems with three separate teams, and the seams between them are where most of the achievable energy savings disappear.

The work below approaches those seams as the unit of analysis: a single objective for the geometry, the supervisory control, and the model that links them.

02Research interests

  1. AI agents for industrial energy control

    LLM-based agents that read plant documentation, reason over sensor streams, and drive equipment through an ontology layer down to TCP / UDP and the PLC. Each agent is designed and validated against a physics-based digital twin of the target plant before it touches hardware. The aim is auditable supervisory control with explicit constraint handling, not a chatbot over historian data.

  2. Physics-based digital twins of industrial plants

    First-principles models of energy-consuming equipment and processes (Simulink, Modelica, CFD-derived reduced-order models, multiscale couplings), calibrated against field measurements. They have to respond honestly to a change in geometry and a change in supervisory policy, so design and control can argue on the same evidence.

  3. Energy efficiency of manufacturing processes

    Process-scale modeling and control of energy-intensive operations: battery electrode drying, industrial HVAC, steel casting. Measurement-driven digital twins and energy-based KPIs that put process designs and operating strategies on the same axis.

  4. Codesign of thermal systems

    Topology optimization of heat sinks, heat exchangers, and thermal insulators, paired with the operating policy that will run them. The older track in the lab, now advanced primarily through student supervision; the “what to build” side of Codesign.

03Ongoing projects

Active · 2024 –

LLM-based AI Agent for HVAC Control

Autonomous supervisory control of HVAC for a battery-manufacturing dry room. The agent talks to a domain ontology, which talks to PLCs over TCP / UDP. Quality and safety constraints stay in the lower control layer; the LLM reasons over deviations and operator intent rather than closing the loop directly.

Active · 2023 –

Digital Twin of a Desiccant Rotor HVAC

A Simulink digital twin of a desiccant-rotor air-handling system, calibrated against field measurements. The current study evaluates control strategies on the twin and shows roughly 20% energy savings against the as-built operating policy.

04Open questions

  1. When does codesigning equipment and operation beat doing them in sequence, and how do we know in advance, before the hardware is built?

  2. How faithful does a physics-based digital twin need to be before its verdict can replace a real plant in a codesign decision? Where does the boundary between trustworthy simulation and “just run it on hardware” sit?

  3. Can an LLM-based agent be made auditable enough to run an industrial line under hard quality and safety constraints, without humans in the loop on every decision?

  4. What is the right interface between an optimal geometry and the policy that will operate it: a learned model, a reduced-order surrogate, or something the controller itself updates online?

  5. How much of energy-intensive manufacturing’s waste is structural, baked into how plants are commissioned, versus operational and recoverable through better supervisory control?
